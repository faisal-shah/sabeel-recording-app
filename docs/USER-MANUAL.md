# Sabeel Class Recordings — User Manual

*For app version 0.2.0 · July 2026*

Welcome! This guide explains everything the Sabeel Class Recordings app can do —
from listening to your first class to taking attendance and tracking who has
caught up. It is organized by role: **students** start with Part 1; **staff**
(managers and admins) add Part 2; **admins** add Part 3. Part 4 is a quick "How
do I…?" index you can skim any time.

The app works the same on the website
(<https://sabeel-class-recordings.web.app>) and the Android app — same account,
same data, live everywhere. Every screen in this guide is shown twice: once **on
a phone** and once **on a computer**, so you can recognize it wherever you are.

---

## The big picture (2 minutes)

1. **Recordings are audio only.** Each one is the sound of one class meeting — no
   video. They stream in the app; nothing is downloaded to fill up your phone,
   and each play link is freshly issued and expires, so recordings stay inside
   the app.
2. **The app is mostly for catching up on a class you missed.** Staff take
   **attendance** for each meeting. If you were **absent** (or **excused**), the
   recording of that meeting becomes *required listening* for you and appears on
   your home screen. If you were **present**, you're not on the hook — you may
   still listen, but nothing is due.
3. **You mark your own listening complete.** The app saves your place and shows
   how much you've heard, but *you* decide when you're done — it is on your
   honor. (The one rule: you have to have pressed play at least once.)
4. **Staff can see who has caught up.** For every recording there is a ledger:
   of the students who missed the class, who has finished and who is overdue —
   plus, for reference, which attendees listened anyway. Staff can export it and,
   with a written reason, mark a completion on someone's behalf.
5. **Everything important is written down.** Approvals, overrides and edits are
   recorded in an audit history that admins can review.

That's the whole system. Everything below is detail.

## 1.2 Your listening list

This is your home screen — **Your listening**, the recordings you need to catch
up on because you missed the class, most urgent first. It's grouped so nothing
slips past you:

- **Overdue** — required listening whose due date has passed. Shown in red.
- **Due soon** — required, with a due date still ahead.
- **Completed** — recordings you've already marked done.

Each card shows the recording's title, which course it's from, and its due date
(or **Completed**). Tap any card to open it and listen.

If a recording has *no* due date, it's still required, but it waits quietly here
without nagging you — it will show without a date rather than as overdue.

You only ever see your *own* list here. Whether you were marked present, absent,
or excused is between the teacher and the attendance sheet; all you see is what
you need to listen to.

## 1.3 Listening to a recording

<div class="pair">
<figure class="ph"><img src="manual/img/03-player-phone.png" alt="Player screen on a phone"><figcaption>On a phone</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="wd"><img src="manual/img/03-player-desktop.png" alt="Player screen on a computer"><figcaption>On a computer</figcaption></figure>
</div>

The player has everything you'd expect from a podcast app:

- **The big round button** plays and pauses.
- The two side buttons **skip back 15 seconds** and **skip ahead 30 seconds** —
  handy for re-hearing a point or moving past a pause.
- **1× / 1.25× / 1.5× / 2×** change the speed without changing the pitch.
- Drag the **progress bar** to jump anywhere. The times below it show how far in
  you are and how much is left.

A bar shows roughly how much of the recording you've heard. **Your place is saved
automatically**, so you can stop on your phone on the bus and pick up on your
laptop at home — it remembers where you were.

If the teacher left a note, it appears under **About this recording** — often
"focus on this part" or "we'll discuss this next week."

## 1.4 Marking a recording complete

The **Mark complete** button is at the bottom of the player. Tap it when you've
finished listening, and the recording moves to your **Completed** section.

A few things worth knowing:

- **It's on your honor.** The app doesn't force you to listen to every second —
  it trusts you to mark complete when you're genuinely done. The listening bar
  is there to help *you* keep your place, not to police you.
- **You do have to press play first.** The button only unlocks once you've
  started the recording — you can't mark something complete you've never opened.
- **Changed your mind?** You can open a completed recording again and listen
  more; marking complete isn't a locked door.

## 1.5 Browsing all your recordings

<div class="pair">
<figure class="ph"><img src="manual/img/04-browse-all-phone.png" alt="Browse all recordings on a phone"><figcaption>On a phone</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="wd"><img src="manual/img/04-browse-all-desktop.png" alt="Browse all recordings on a computer"><figcaption>On a computer</figcaption></figure>
</div>

Your home screen shows what you *need* to catch up on. But being enrolled in a
course opens **every** published recording in it to you — including the meetings
you attended. Tap **Browse all recordings** to see them, grouped by course.

Use this to re-hear a session you were at, or to listen to something that isn't
required but you'd like to review. Listening here is exactly the same — the same
player, the same saved place.

## 1.6 Listening on a spotty connection

The app is built to survive a bad signal. If you mark a recording complete while
you're offline or your connection drops, the app **holds onto that** and syncs it
the moment you're back online — even if you close the app in between. You may
briefly see a "pending" state; that just means "saved on this device, not yet
sent." You don't have to do anything; it sorts itself out.

Streaming the audio itself does need a connection, so for a long class on the go,
a stable network (or Wi-Fi before you leave) is your friend.

# Part 2 — For staff

*Managers and admins both use this section. A few actions are admin-only and are
marked; managers see only the courses they've been assigned to.*

## 2.1 Signing in and getting approved

Staff sign in differently from students. On the sign-in screen, use **Sign in
with Google** with your Sabeel Google account.

**The first time, you'll wait for approval.** New staff accounts start as
*pending* — an administrator has to approve you and choose your role before you
can do anything. Once they do, sign in again and you're in. (If you're the very
first administrator, that step was done for you during setup.)

## 2.4 Sessions, attendance, and adding a recording

<div class="pair">
<figure class="ph"><img src="manual/img/16-sessions-phone.png" alt="Sessions list on a phone"><figcaption>On a phone</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="wd"><img src="manual/img/16-sessions-desktop.png" alt="Sessions list on a computer"><figcaption>On a computer</figcaption></figure>
</div>

A **session** is one class meeting. Create one for each meeting — give it a
title, its date, and an optional due date for absentees. Each session card shows
at a glance whether **attendance** has been taken and whether a **recording** has
been added, so you can see what's still outstanding.

Open a session to work with it:

<div class="pair">
<figure class="ph"><img src="manual/img/17-session-detail-phone.png" alt="Session detail with attendance on a phone"><figcaption>On a phone</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="wd"><img src="manual/img/17-session-detail-desktop.png" alt="Session detail with attendance on a computer"><figcaption>On a computer</figcaption></figure>
</div>

**Take attendance.** For each enrolled student, choose **Present**, **Absent**,
or **Excused**, then tap **Submit attendance**. Everyone starts as *Present*, so
often you only flip the few who missed; **Mark all absent** is a one-tap shortcut
for the "everyone must listen to this one" case. You can re-submit a corrected
attendance later and everything updates automatically.

- **Present** students were there — the recording is not required for them.
- **Absent** and **Excused** students missed the content, so the recording
  becomes their required listening. (The two differ only in your reports;
  excused students are still expected to catch up.)

**Add the recording.** In the same screen, **Upload audio…** or import it from
Zoom. It's **audio only** — up to 300 MB, though a two-hour class is normally
well under 30 MB. (Uploading audio rather than a video file is the single biggest
thing that keeps this app free to run.) The recording takes its title, date, and
notes from the session, so you don't retype them.

Each recording moves through a few states:

- **Draft** — added, not yet visible to students. You can replace its audio
  freely.
- **Published** — live. Enrolled students can listen, and the absent/excused
  students are now on the hook for it.
- **Needs attention** — flagged for a problem (for example, a Zoom import that
  needs review). Publish is blocked until it has audio.

## 2.5 How a recording becomes required listening

Here's the rule that runs the whole ledger:

> **A recording is required listening for the students who were marked absent or
> excused for its session** — assigned automatically once the recording is
> **published** and its attendance has been **submitted**.

You never assign students one by one; the attendance sheet is the assignment. If
you correct attendance — flip someone from present to absent, or the reverse —
the obligations update to match, and any listening or completion already recorded
is kept.

- **A due date is optional**, set on the session (or leave it blank). With one,
  absentees see "due soon" then "overdue"; without one, it's required but never
  overdue.
- **A student who joins late** wasn't on the roster when earlier attendance was
  taken, so they are **not** retroactively put on the hook for those older
  sessions — accountability starts at enrollment. If you *do* want a latecomer to
  catch up on an earlier session, open that session, mark them absent, and
  re-submit attendance; they'll be assigned it.
- **Unpublishing** a recording takes it back out of required listening.

## 2.6 The recording ledger — who has caught up

<div class="pair">
<figure class="ph"><img src="manual/img/18-recording-ledger-phone.png" alt="Recording ledger on a phone"><figcaption>On a phone</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="wd"><img src="manual/img/18-recording-ledger-desktop.png" alt="Recording ledger on a computer"><figcaption>On a computer</figcaption></figure>
</div>

Open a published recording's **Listening progress** (from its session) to see
exactly where things stand. Up top, four counts: **accountable** (the students on
the hook — those who were absent or excused), **complete**, **incomplete**, and
**overdue**.

The screen has two parts:

- **The accountable list** — absent and excused students (excused ones are
  badged), each with how much they've listened, when they last listened, and
  their status: **Complete**, **Incomplete**, or **Overdue**. The **filter
  chips** — *Not complete / Overdue / All* — open on **Not complete**, so you land
  on exactly the people who still need a nudge.
- **Attendees** — the students who were *present*. They were there, so the
  recording isn't required for them and they're never overdue; their listening is
  shown for reference only, in case you want to see who revisited it.

**Export CSV** downloads the accountable rows — the same rows, the same filter —
as a spreadsheet, for records or a follow-up.

## 2.7 Overriding a completion

<div class="pair">
<figure class="ph"><img src="manual/img/19-override-form-phone.png" alt="Override form on a phone"><figcaption>On a phone</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="wd"><img src="manual/img/19-override-form-desktop.png" alt="Override form on a computer"><figcaption>On a computer</figcaption></figure>
</div>

Sometimes the ledger and reality differ — a student caught up in person, or
listened on someone else's device. Tap **Override** on their row to set the
status yourself:

- Enter a **reason** — it's **required**, and it's written into the audit history
  so there's always a record of why. "Caught up with the teacher one-on-one" is a
  good example.
- Choose **Mark complete** or **Mark not complete**.

An override **wins over the student's own mark**. If you mark someone complete and
they later fiddle with their own status, your override stands. To undo it, open
the row again and **Remove override** — the status falls back to whatever the
student's own listening says. Every override, and every removal, is audited.

## 2.8 One student's ledger

From a course's **Roster**, tap a student to see *their* view: the recordings
they're required to catch up on in that course, each with its status, the same
action-first filters, and its own **Export CSV**. It's the per-person counterpart
to the per-recording ledger in 2.6 — the right screen when a parent or student
asks "where do I stand?"

## 3.1 Approving staff

<figure class="ph"><img src="manual/img/12-staff-approvals-phone.png" alt="Staff approvals on a phone"><figcaption>On a phone</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="wd"><img src="manual/img/12-staff-approvals-desktop.png" alt="Staff approvals on a computer"><figcaption>On a computer</figcaption></figure>

New staff who sign in with Google land in **Waiting for approval**. For each, you
choose the role as you approve:

- **Approve as manager** — they can run the courses you later assign to them.
- **Approve as admin** — full access, including approving other staff.

Below, **Everyone else** lists active staff. Your own account is marked "This is
you" — you can't change your own role or access, which stops an admin from
accidentally locking themselves out.

Only staff on the institute's own Google domain can be approved at all — the app
checks this on the server, not just in the browser.

## 3.2 Managers are scoped course by course

A manager can't see anything until you give them a course. Do that on the
**course page** (Section 2.3), under **Managers** — add them there, and they'll
see that course's sessions, recordings, ledgers, attendance, and audit, and
nothing else. Putting a course in a cohort a manager works in does *not* grant
access; the assignment is always the specific course.

## 3.3 The audit history

<div class="pair">
<figure class="ph"><img src="manual/img/22-audit-phone.png" alt="Audit history on a phone"><figcaption>On a phone</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="wd"><img src="manual/img/22-audit-desktop.png" alt="Audit history on a computer"><figcaption>On a computer</figcaption></figure>
</div>

The **audit history** is the running record of consequential actions — staff
approvals, attendance submissions, completion overrides (with their reasons),
recording edits — newest first. Each entry shows *who* did *what*, *when*, and the
details that matter.

You can reach it two ways: **globally** from the staff home (admins, all
courses), or **scoped to one course** from that course's page (managers see their
own courses' history). It's there so that "who marked this complete, and why?"
always has an answer.

## 3.4 Disable and archive — don't delete

The app is built around **reversible** actions:

- **Disable** a student or staff member to switch off access while keeping their
  history.
- **Unpublish** a recording to pull it from students without losing it.
- **Archive** a course when a term ends — its history stays intact, and you
  decide whether students may still listen to it.

Permanent deletion exists, but it's deliberately admin-only, asks for strong
confirmation, and is audited. Reach for disable, unpublish, or archive first.

## Troubleshooting

**I signed in but I'm stuck on an "approval" screen.** *(Staff)* New accounts wait
for an admin to approve them. The screen updates by itself the moment they do — no
refresh needed. If you're stuck, contact your administrator.

**A student can't sign in.** Check the email address matches the one on their
account exactly, and use **Resend password link** (Students screen) so they can
set a fresh password. New students have no password until they use that link.

**The audio won't play.** Streaming needs a working connection — try switching
between Wi-Fi and mobile data. Play links refresh automatically, so simply
reopening the recording usually clears a stale one.

**A student says they shouldn't be on the hook for a recording.** Check that
session's attendance — required listening follows it. If they were there, mark
them **Present** and re-submit; the obligation drops (and any listening they did
is kept). If they genuinely missed it, that's exactly what the required listing
is for.

**I marked something complete but it still shows incomplete.** If you were
offline, the app is holding your mark and will sync it once you're back online;
it may briefly show as pending. If a *teacher* has overridden your status, their
mark takes precedence — that's by design.

**A manager can't see a course.** Managers only see courses they're assigned to.
An admin adds them under **Managers** on the specific course page — being in the
cohort isn't enough.

**Where did a recording go?** It may have been **unpublished** (back to draft) or
its course **archived**. Both are reversible — check the session or the course
settings.
